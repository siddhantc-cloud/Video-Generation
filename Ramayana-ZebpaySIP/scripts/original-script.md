# **The Squirrel in Ram Setu (Small Investments Create Big Wealth)**

## 

## **SCENE 1: THE PURPOSE**

**Visual:** Grand wide shot. Rama standing at the shore, Dhanush in hand, hair flowing in the wind, gazing toward the distant horizon where Lanka lies, hidden beyond the sea.

**Narrator (grand, reverent, full of weight):** Across this ocean waited a battle for dharma. Sita had to be brought home. Ravana had to fall.

**Visual:** The vanara sena gathered behind Rama, thousands of them, ready, determined.

**Narrator (building, resolute):** And to get there, an army would build what had never been built before. A bridge across the sea.

**Visual:** The first boulder lifted, carried, hurled into the water with a thunderous splash. Work begins in full force.

**Narrator (awe, scale in the voice):** Stone by stone. Boulder by boulder. Each one marked with Ram’s name, and his faith.

## 

## **SCENE 2: THE SQUIRREL ARRIVES**

**Visual:** Amid the scale and noise of the construction, a small squirrel appears at the edge of the shore, watching the vanaras work.

**Narrator (softer, curious, a small shift in scale):** And in the middle of all that grandeur, something much smaller had also arrived.

**Visual:** The squirrel picks up a tiny pebble in its mouth, struggles slightly with the weight, scurries up to the bridge, and places it carefully into a gap between the boulders.

**Narrator (warm, simple):** It couldn't carry boulders. So it carried what it could.

## **SCENE 3: THE MOCKERY**

**Visual:** The squirrel arrives at the bridge, a pebble in its mouth, and tries to place it between two boulders.

**Visual:** A vanara notices, stops, and, almost performing for the squirrel now, hoists a massive boulder onto his shoulder, flexing, looking right at it.

**Narrator (playful, like watching someone show off on purpose):** Of course, one of the vanaras noticed. And… decided to make a point of it.

**Visual:** Vanar laughs and picks up two boulders, one in each hand, grinning down at the squirrel.

**Narrator (amused, building):** He made sure everyone laughed with him.

**Visual:** A baby vanara walks past, casually carrying a decent-sized stone, not even trying.

**Narrator (light chuckle in the voice, almost teasing):** Even the babies were outdoing it without breaking a sweat.

**Visual:** The lead vanara picks up the squirrel's pebble between two fingers, holds it up right in front of the squirrel's face, and flicks it away.

**Narrator (flat, deadpan):** Naturally, nobody clapped.

## **SCENE 4: Lord RAMA NOTICES**

**Visual:** Lord Rama stands at the shore, bow in hand, watching the bridge take shape. His eyes move past the boulders, past the boasting vanaras, and land on the squirrel.

**Narrator (slower, warmer, almost reverent):** But someone else was watching too.

**Visual:** Rama walks toward the squirrel. The laughter fades as the vanaras notice him approaching.

**Narrator (quiet, unhurried):** And he wasn't looking at the boulders.

**Visual:** Rama kneels. Picks up the squirrel gently in his palm.

**Narrator (soft, full of feeling):** He was looking at the only one who showed up without being asked to.

**Visual:** Rama strokes the squirrel's back with two fingers. The vanaras watch in silence now, their boasting gone.

**Narrator (gentle, final, like closing a thought):** Some bridges are built with strength. Some are built with heart. The wise ones know you need both.

## **SCENE 5: THE BRIDGE GETS COMPLETED**

**Visual:** Time passes, quick repeated cuts of the squirrel returning to the shore, picking up a pebble, placing it. Some days the tide is calm. Some days the waves are rough and it struggles to even reach the shore. It still goes.

**Narrator (steady, deliberate):** Some days the sea was calm. Some days it wasn't. The squirrel never waited for the right day.

**Visual:** The bridge growing, boulders on one side, a steadily lengthening line of pebbles wedged between them on the other, each one roughly the same size.

**Narrator (building quietly):** It didn't matter if the tide was high or low that day. The pebble went in anyway.

**Visual:** Camera pushes into the gap where the pebbles sit, now load-bearing, indistinguishable from the boulders around them.

**Narrator (landing it, slower):** One pebble, placed once, means nothing. The same pebble, placed again and again, regardless of the day, that's what holds a bridge together.

## **SCENE 6: THE PARALLEL**

**Visual:** Clean transition. No mythology visuals, calm space for the thought to land.

**Narrator (plainspoken, slightly slower pace):** Most people wait for the right time to invest. The market down, wait. The market up, too late. The squirrel never checked the tide before showing up.

Pause.

**Narrator (warm but firm):** It showed up the same way, every time, and let the average take care of the rest.

## **SCENE 7: OUTRO**

**Visual:** The completed bridge, golden light, wide shot. The squirrel sitting on one of its own pebbles, now fused permanently into the structure.

**Narrator (direct to camera, warm, plainspoken):** You don't need to time the market. You need to keep showing up, same amount, every time. That's not a squirrel's trick. That's SIP.
